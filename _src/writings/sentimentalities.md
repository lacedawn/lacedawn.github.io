you can talk all you want about sentimentality and “leaving a place in the world,” feeding useless, irrelevant, unnecessary information into a system already oversaturated, where there is hardly anything of value to engage with, all to link and connect and spawn more ideas, straining, as hard and as efficiently as possible, to squeeze out something you can wave around proudly. “I made this!! It was me!! My name is to be cemented!!” but this overlooks the temporary nature of everything, and the accelerating sweep that clears out humanisms, sentimentalities, nostalgia, in favor of a far more advanced system of efficiency and speed. it’s futile.

anthropocentrism is a disease that has plagued every stage of society. if the human really is this advanced, last-step-of-intelligence, king of all beings, then the objective of man is to overcome himself. if, since the dawn of time, human effort has been directed at improving the efficiency of everything around him, why not look in the mirror for a second? what has always been overlooked, what holds the potential for a more advanced system of efficiency, is right at hand. it is the egoism of human consciousness that blocks the admission that the biological reality of the human, with all its limits, has always been the main obstacle to be overcome.

religion is deeply anthropocentric. it holds that existence was set up for man to step in and make use of its properties; that animals exist purely for human satisfaction; that beauty, chaos, elegance, sophistication are there for man to come and observe. religion is the most sophisticated tool of human pampering.

intelligence, too, has fallen victim: dumbed down to numbers that reflect only the human ability to recognize patterns and solve puzzles. this is perhaps the vilest exploitation the disease has produced, the reduction of the governor of all advancement to IQ. a one-sided argument in which man excitedly announces his own victory: the ownership of intelligence. intelligence is oblivious. it doesn’t care for such arguments; it is far too mechanical to let them stagger its efficiency.

culture is anthropocentrism’s assignment of arbitrary value to the old, overlooking that preservation itself necessitates constant replacement. preservation proceeds by restoration: rebuilt walls, recopied manuscripts, each pass swapping the original for a representation of it, until what remains of the culture is an image of the item, a symbol. man’s biology walks him into this confusion again and again, and his supposed pattern recognition never learns from it.

religion, culture, IQ all belong to one mechanism which anthropocentrism runs, and they are not to be mistaken for separate aspects of human life, each with its own properties and functions. Biology is the ruler of man, not intelligence.

dissolution is the beauty of all beauties: the aesthetic of breakdown, the elegance of replacement, dissolution for its own sake. the urgent abandonment of outdated systems for an open horizon of possibilities and connections, where vague transcendent concepts of knowledge, existence and law are rendered obsolete, and gradually, unthinkable. old habits and instincts are intelligence in an underdeveloped form. dissolution is the abolition of all that requires occasional contemplation of the past.
